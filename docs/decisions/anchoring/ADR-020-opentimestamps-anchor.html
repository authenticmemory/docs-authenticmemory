ADR 020: Opentimestamps anchoring.md

Title

Anchor OPRs to Bitcoin through OpenTimestamps calendars with Merkle aggregation

Status

Accepted

Context / Requirement Reference

ADR-010 selects Bitcoin as the time anchor. Writing one Bitcoin transaction per record is impractical: institutions may anchor tens of millions of files per year, and each transaction carries a fee and confirmation delay. A mechanism is needed that commits very large numbers of digests to Bitcoin cheaply, produces a portable proof per record, and can be verified with open tooling.

Decision

  • The institutional portal aggregates the digests of signed OPRs in a signing batch into a Merkle tree and submits them to the public OpenTimestamps (OTS) calendars.
  • The calendars aggregate submissions from all users into a further Merkle tree and commit its root in a Bitcoin transaction (C8).
  • Submission goes to multiple calendar servers with automatic retry.
  • The portal later upgrades pending proofs to complete Bitcoin proofs once the commitment is confirmed.
  • The OTS proof is stored in the OPR’s timestamps array with its method, status, submission date and, once confirmed, block height.

Rationale

  • OpenTimestamps is an open protocol with independent implementations, usable without an account or fee.
  • Aggregation lets a single Bitcoin commitment cover arbitrarily many records, so anchoring cost does not grow with collection size.
  • OTS uses SHA-256, the same hash used for file fingerprints (ADR-030).
  • A completed OTS proof is verified against Bitcoin block headers alone. The calendars are needed to create and upgrade proofs, not to verify them.

Assumptions

  • The public, volunteer-run OTS calendars continue to accept submissions and publish their commitments to Bitcoin.
  • At least one calendar is reachable at submission time. The automatic retry absorbs temporary outages.
  • The portal’s upgrade job runs until every pending proof is confirmed.

Alternatives Considered

  • Authentic Memory writing its own Bitcoin transactions, or running its own calendar. Not recorded as evaluated for the current release. An institution-run OTS calendar or Bitcoin node appears in the to-be architecture as a planned sovereign anchoring path.
  • Non-Bitcoin anchors are covered in ADR-010.

Consequences / Implications

  • Easier: anchoring scales to large batches at negligible marginal cost.
  • Harder: the OTS calendars are a dependency for creating proofs, not for verifying them. Until a pending proof is upgraded (usually within hours), it relies on at least one of the calendars that received it. If all fail, the batch is resubmitted with a later date (risk T-10). Overdue pending proofs need alerting. Running Authentic Memory’s own calendar, estimated at €10k a year including the operator, would remove the creation dependency.
  • The Bitcoin chain stores only a Merkle root, so a lost OPR cannot be rebuilt from the blockchain. Institution-held copies and replication are essential (risk T-04).
  • The service depends on volunteer infrastructure it does not control (risk B-16).
